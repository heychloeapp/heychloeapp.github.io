Privacy policy

hey chloe · Updated November 10, 2026

What you log is kept on your iPhone. hey chloe has no account. Your foods, workouts, body measurements, sleep, cycle and notes are kept in the app on your phone (and your Apple Watch). Two things leave the phone, both explained below: your lifts with no name (Share lift data anonymously, which you can turn off) and, only if you turn it on, a locked cloud backup that we can't open. We never sell anything and show no ads.

Apple Health

With your permission, hey chloe reads from Apple Health (for example weight, height, waist, sleep, energy burned, heart rate during workouts, and period days if you turn on Cycle) and saves to Apple Health what you log (food nutrients and workouts). What it reads stays on your phone. Health data is never used for advertising and never included in usage statistics. It leaves your phone only if you say yes: your body weight with your shared lifts, or inside your locked cloud backup.

Anonymous usage statistics

To find bugs and see which features are used, the app sends anonymous usage events to PostHog (servers in the United States):

  • which screens are opened and which actions are taken, like "a food was logged" or "a workout finished", in fixed words and small counts;
  • errors and slowdowns (where in the app they happened, never what was on screen);
  • the app and iOS version, the kind of device and its screen size, and a random ID made when the app is installed.

They never include what you ate, any weight or number you typed, names, notes, anything from Apple Health, anything about your cycle, or your location. The random ID isn't tied to your name, email or Apple account. PostHog is set not to look up locations and to discard IP addresses.

Share lift data anonymously

To make the strength levels fit real people, the app sends the sets you finish to hey chloe's own server (Supabase, servers in the United States), a day after each workout. This is on by default and can be turned off in Gym Settings, Share lift data anonymously, which also shows what is sent. Turning it off stops sharing from then on.

  • Sent: for each finished set of a built-in exercise, the exercise, the weight, the reps or time, the week it was done, its order in the workout, which app it was logged in or imported from, and random IDs made on your phone that say which sets were one workout and came from the same phone.
  • Only with your yes: your gender (the one you picked in the app), your age in five-year steps (like 35 to 39) and your body weight near that workout, to the kilogram. The app asks once; you can say yes or no later in the same place. A no stops them from then on.
  • Never sent: your name, email or Apple account, exercises you made yourself, notes, workout names, times of day, heart rate, food, sleep, cycle or anything else from Apple Health.
  • Nothing is sent for anyone under 18.

The random ID isn't tied to your name, your device or the usage statistics' ID. To have what your phone already sent deleted from the server, write to CONTACT_EMAIL with that ID, which the same page shows (a tap copies it).

Cloud backup

Only if you turn it on (App settings, Backup), the app backs up all its data once a day to hey chloe's server, with your Apple Health data if Include Apple Health data is on. Each backup is locked on your phone before it leaves, with a key kept in your iCloud Keychain, so neither we nor anyone who reached the server can open it. The server keeps your three newest. A new iPhone signed in to your Apple ID can restore them. Delete cloud backups, in the same place, deletes them all from the server.

When the app goes online

  • Barcode scans: the barcode number is looked up in USDA FoodData Central and Open Food Facts.
  • Gym machine codes: the link on the machine's sticker is opened to read its name.
  • Exercise videos: tutorial videos come from YouTube (or the site of a link you add), and that site's own privacy policy applies.

Nothing else about you is sent with these.

What you choose to send

Shares, exports and text copied for a chat assistant go only where you send them. An exported backup is a file you save wherever you choose. Your iPhone's own iCloud backup may include the app's data, as set in your iPhone's settings.

Deleting your data

Each section's Settings has Erase, and deleting the app removes everything it kept on your phone. Cloud backups are deleted from the server with Delete cloud backups; to have shared lifts deleted, write to us. Data hey chloe saved to Apple Health can be removed from the app's Settings or in the Health app.

Children

hey chloe is not meant for children under 13.
